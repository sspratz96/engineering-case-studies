## Executive Summary

Two to four short paragraphs: the problem, what was built, the main engineering idea and the most important result. A reader who stops here should understand the case.

---

# Related Repository

Optional. Only for public repositories. One line on what the reader will find there and how it relates to this case.

---

# Context

The organization (by industry, size and country, never by name), my role, the period and the constraints that shaped the decisions.

---

# The Problem

What was wrong, for whom, and why it mattered. Describe the situation before any solution existed.

---

# Requirements or Design Principles

The criteria the solution had to meet. Keep them short; each one should explain at least one later decision.

---

# Options Considered

Optional, but recommended when a real alternative was evaluated. For each option: what it was, and why it was chosen or rejected.

---

# Architecture

<p align="center">
  <img src="./diagrams/svg/diagram-name.svg" alt="Describe what the diagram shows" width="950">
</p>

How the system works, from input to output. Describe components by function before naming tools.

---

# Key Engineering Decisions

## 1. Decision Stated as an Action

What was decided, the alternative, and why.

---

# Trade-Offs and Risks

What the design gives up, and when it would stop being the right design.

---

# Results

## Baseline

What existed before. If nothing existed, say so.

## What Exists Now

Capabilities that did not exist before.

## Direct Comparisons

Only real before-and-after measurements, with their source. Omit this section if there are none.

---

# Lessons Learned

## 1. Lesson Stated as a Principle

One or two sentences tied to something that happened in this project.

---

# What I Would Do Differently Today

Concrete changes, each one linked to a limitation or lesson above.

---

# Next Steps

Optional. Only for cases that are still evolving.
